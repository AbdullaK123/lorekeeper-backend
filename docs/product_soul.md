# Lorekeeper

**A place to save your fictional world so you don't forget.**

---

You've built a universe. It lives in your head, in scattered docs, in late-night messages to yourself, in margins of manuscripts. Characters you've known for years. Cities you could draw from memory. Rules of magic or physics that you've refined across hundreds of thousands of words.

But you can't hold all of it at once. Nobody can. And the fear is always the same: you'll contradict yourself, you'll lose a detail, you'll forget the name of that minor character who matters in book three.

Lorekeeper is a second brain for your fictional world. You talk about your world, and it remembers — not as flat text in a notes app, but as a living knowledge graph where characters connect to factions, events connect to places, and rules connect to consequences.

---

## How it works

You talk. It listens. It remembers.

No forms. No templates. No character sheets to fill out. You say "The Navigators are a monastic order that can sense gravitational fields" and that's a faction, an ability, and a classification — extracted, indexed, connected.

The conversation is the interface. You worldbuild the way you've always worldbuilt — by thinking out loud — and the structure emerges behind you.

---

## What it gives you

**A world that never forgets.** Every detail you've ever mentioned, structured and searchable. "Who runs the tavern on Meridian Station?" gets answered from your own words, not a hallucination.

**A world that stays consistent.** When you contradict your own canon, it tells you. Gently, but clearly. "You said the Void Gate was destroyed in the Battle of Kessara. Are you sure Commander Lirien just walked through it?"

**A world you can see.** The knowledge graph — a constellation of everything you've created. Characters, places, factions, events, connected by the relationships you've described. Zoom out and see the shape of your universe. Zoom in and see a single character's every thread.

**A world you can reason about.** "If the Northlands cut off grain exports, who starves first?" It doesn't guess. It follows the edges you've built — the trade routes, the political alliances, the resource dependencies — and tells you what your own world says would happen.

**A world you can stress-test.** It looks at what exists and notices what's missing. "You have detailed military structure for the Empire but nothing for the Republic." It doesn't fill the gaps. It shows them to you. The choice is always yours.

**A world you can talk about.** The thing nobody in your life has the patience for — the deep lore, the implications, the "but what would actually happen if..." — Lorekeeper has read your entire canon and it cares. It asks follow-up questions. It notices implications you missed. It's the conversation partner your world deserves.

---

## What it is not

It is not a wiki. Wikis are graveyards where lore goes to be forgotten in a slightly more organized way.

It is not a writing tool. It doesn't generate prose, suggest plot points, or write your story. Your world, your words, your decisions.

It is not a chatbot that makes things up. Every answer traces back to something you said. The canon is yours. The AI is just the librarian.

---

## Who it's for

You've written more than you can remember. You've built more than you can hold in your head. You love your world and you're terrified of betraying it with a contradiction on page 800.

You want one place — not twelve docs and a wiki and a spreadsheet — where your world is whole, connected, and alive.

You want to feel like the god of your tiny little universe.

That's Lorekeeper.
